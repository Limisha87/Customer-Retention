CHURN PREVENTION GUIDELINES

Customer churn means that a customer stops using or paying for a
company's service.

Common churn indicators include:
- Short customer tenure
- Month-to-month contracts
- High monthly charges
- Poor customer support experience
- Technical problems
- Low engagement
- Lack of perceived value


EARLY WARNING

Customers showing multiple churn indicators should be prioritized
for proactive retention activities.

The retention team should review the customer's profile before
contacting the customer.


PERSONALIZED RETENTION

Retention recommendations should consider the customer's individual
circumstances.

For example:

Short tenure:
Provide onboarding and education.

Month-to-month contract:
Explain longer-term contract benefits.

High charges:
Review plan value and available alternatives.

Technical problems:
Provide technical assistance and follow-up.

Poor engagement:
Use relevant communication and customer education.


RETENTION COMMUNICATION

Communication should be helpful and personalized.

Avoid aggressive sales messaging.

The objective is to understand the customer's needs, solve problems,
and improve customer satisfaction.


FOLLOW-UP

After a retention action, the company should monitor the customer
and determine whether the problem has been resolved.

Follow-up is especially important for customers previously identified
as high churn risk.
